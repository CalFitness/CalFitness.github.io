Food logging

Getting the day down in the fewest taps it can honestly take.

  • The daily logOne screen for the day: what you ate, what it cost, and what is left.
  • Food searchOne search across your own foods, the shared catalog, and branded products.
  • Barcode scannerPoint the camera at a packet and log what is actually in it.
  • Custom foodsYour own foods, with your own serving sizes, saved once and reused forever.
  • Recipes and combosLog a meal made of six things as one thing.
  • Portions and household measuresKatori, roti, glass, slice, packet — units people actually use.
  • Meals and meal timesBreakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks — named, timed, and yours to change.
  • Add nextA short list of what you are most likely to eat next, ranked by why.
  • Cook alongFollow a recipe video step by step, then log what you actually made.
  • Describe a dishSay what you ate in plain language and get a checkable breakdown.

Nutrition planning

Deciding what the day should look like before you have to live it.

  • Your daily calorie targetA number derived from your own data, not a formula applied once at signup.
  • Weekly energy budgetThe daily number is a slice of a weekly one, shaped around your real week.
  • Macro targetsProtein, carbs and fat as targets you can reason about, not four rings.
  • Day plannerBuild tomorrow before you get there, then log it as you eat it.
  • Meal programsA multi-day eating plan you can follow, edit, or hand to someone else.
  • Suggestions that know your goalWhat to eat next, chosen for the gap you actually have.
  • Habit-first portionsIf you always take the same amount, the app stops asking.
  • Was that a light day or a short log?One tap that stops a half-logged day from poisoning your averages.

Training

Logging a session while you are in it, with your hands full and a bar to get back to.

  • Start an empty workoutBegin with nothing planned and add exercises as you do them.
  • RoutinesA named session with its exercises, sets and targets, ready to run.
  • Plans and programsA multi-week structure of routines, with a place in it for today.
  • Logging a setWeight, reps and done — in one thumb, without looking closely.
  • Rest timerStarts itself when you finish a set, and follows you out of the app.
  • Live ActivityThe set you are on and the rest you have left, on the iOS lock screen.
  • SupersetsTwo or more exercises alternating in rounds, with one rest per round.
  • Drop setsA top set and its drops recorded as one set, because that is what it is.
  • Timed setsPlanks, carries and holds logged in seconds, with a countdown that runs them.
  • Warm-ups and cool-downsRamp sets that do not pollute your working-set numbers.
  • Last time you did thisWhat you lifted for this exercise last session, on the card while you decide.
  • Personal recordsA record marked on the set that earned it, using the set you actually did.
  • Equipment and your gymTell the app what your gym has, and the numbers stop being fiction.
  • Rep rangesOwn the range before you add weight.
  • Workout settingsUnits, rest defaults, plate maths and haptics, in one place.
  • Apple Watch appLog the set from your wrist, phone in the bag.
  • Browse workouts and exercisesFind something to do, ranked by what you have been neglecting.

Progress and insight

Reading what happened, and refusing to read what did not.

  • Weekly reportA completed week, with the plan on trial rather than you.
  • Monthly reportBehaviour is the weekly question. Outcome is the monthly one.
  • The last seven daysA running view that does not care where Monday is.
  • The home railCards that have to earn their place, and a quiet home screen when none do.
  • What you actually burnMaintenance measured from your own log and scale, not predicted by a formula.
  • Weight trendThe line through the noise, not the number on the scale this morning.
  • Body measurementsTape, calipers, scale or scan — recorded with their uncertainty visible.
  • SkinfoldsCaliper sites recorded properly, with the body-fat estimate they support.
  • Progress photosA set of angles on a date, comparable because they are the same angles.
  • Readiness body mapWhat is recovered and what is not, per muscle, on a body you can read.
  • 3D muscle mapA body you can rotate, with the muscles you trained lit up.
  • Exercise historyEvery set you have done of one movement, in one place.
  • Streaks and consistencyHow reliably you show up, without turning a missed day into a catastrophe.

Social and accountability

Other people, in the amount that helps and no more.

  • The feedWhat the people you follow have been doing, without the performance.
  • ProfilesWhat you have done, shown to the people you want to show it to.
  • Visibility controlsWho can see what, enforced at the database rather than in the interface.
  • Cheers and commentsEncouragement, including from people without an account.
  • ChallengesA shared goal with a start, an end, and a scoreboard.
  • TeamsChallenges where the score is collective.
  • PactsA commitment between two people, with the terms written down.
  • AccountabilitySomeone who sees whether you did it.
  • Share cardsA card of a session, a record or a month — honest about what it claims.
  • JourneyA transformation challenge: commit to a window, check in, show the result.
  • Saved workoutsShelve a session you liked, then run it with your own weights.
  • Coaching connectionsPlan for someone else — a client, or a family member — without sharing a login.

Libraries and integrations

The catalogues underneath, and the things CalFitness talks to.

  • Exercise libraryOver a thousand exercises with demonstrations, muscles and equipment.
  • Custom exercisesAdd the machine your gym has that nobody else does.
  • Food catalogueMulti-source nutrition data, reconciled rather than averaged.
  • Apple HealthWeight and activity in, workouts out, nothing you did not agree to.
  • ChatGPT connectorBuild routines and meal plans from a conversation, into your real account.
  • AI workout coachDescribe what you want and get a routine you can edit, in the app.
  • AI meal coachTalk through a day or a week of eating and have it land in your plan.
  • Your dataYours to take, yours to delete, and deletion means deletion.
